# Wet Cold

**Planning context:** Rain, sleet or wet snow combines with low temperatures and limited drying. Wind and repeated stops can turn damp clothing into a major source of heat loss. Climate labels here overlap: a maritime route can also be wet cold, and an alpine route can cross Mediterranean foothills.

## Weather characteristics

Rain, sleet or wet snow combines with low temperatures and limited drying. Wind and repeated stops can turn damp clothing into a major source of heat loss.

## Main risks

Wet insulation, cold hands, condensation inside a shell, and progressively wetter gear over successive days.

## Recommended layering strategy

Wear a light base and adjustable moving insulation. Put on the rain barrier before the layers beneath it become soaked; vent it before sweat accumulates. Keep a separate dry static layer accessible for stops. This is a function-based starting point; no fixed temperature limit is inferred from a brand or fabric name. [Layering functions and exertion](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/layering-basics.html).

## Seasonal considerations

Spring and autumn often call for frequent transitions. Winter can move the problem toward snow and freezing, but a forecast near freezing still needs a wet-weather plan.
