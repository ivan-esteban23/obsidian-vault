# Using Your Second Brain

## Save what matters

Open the command palette with Ctrl+P and choose:

- **QuickAdd: Save a thought** for ideas, things you learned, useful links, and conversation summaries. These go to [[Knowledge Inbox]] with a timestamp.
- **QuickAdd: Save a task** for an action you want to remember. These appear in [[Daily Life Hub]] alongside your other tasks.
- **QuickAdd: Journal entry** for experiences and reflections. These go to the same daily note that Calendar opens.

For sources, keep the URL and write why it matters to you. Spoken conversations and chats in other apps must be summarized, pasted, or transcribed into a note before they become part of this collection.

## Find and connect

Use Omnisearch or Obsidian Search to find words, names, and phrases across saved notes. Add internal note links when two ideas are connected. For visual thinking, use [[Brainstorming Ideas]].

Once a week, review the inbox, connect useful entries to existing notes, and choose an idea to develop in [[Writing Practice]].

## Ask questions about your notes

Copilot is installed but disabled to conserve RAM. Capture, daily notes, links, and ordinary search remain available. If you later want AI answers, enable Copilot under **Settings → Community plugins**, then configure a provider and credential directly in Obsidian. A cloud model receives the question and any note context you send to it.

Then open **Copilot: Open Copilot Quick Chat window**, attach the relevant notes, and ask a specific question. For example:

- What writing exercises have I saved? Cite the notes.
- Summarize the ideas in my Knowledge Inbox and suggest what to develop next.
- Compare these album notes using only their documented sources.

Start by testing against a known note and open its citation to verify the answer. If the notes do not contain an answer, the assistant should say so. Saving a note keeps it available for retrieval; it does not guarantee that an AI will retrieve every relevant passage.
