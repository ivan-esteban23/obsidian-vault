# Mediterranean

**Planning context:** Central Chile has a characteristic winter-wet, summer-dry pattern, moderated near the Pacific. Inland heat, coastal humidity and mountain elevation create distinct clothing needs. Climate labels here overlap: a maritime route can also be wet cold, and an alpine route can cross Mediterranean foothills.

## Weather characteristics

Central Chile has a characteristic winter-wet, summer-dry pattern, moderated near the Pacific. Inland heat, coastal humidity and mountain elevation create distinct clothing needs.

## Main risks

Heat and solar exposure, unreliable seasonal water, excessive worn insulation on ascent, and carrying a valley-temperature assumption into the Andes.

## Recommended layering strategy

In summer, prioritize breathable sun coverage and ventilation. Pack a wind layer, forecast-appropriate storm protection and enough static insulation for the highest or most exposed part of the route. Winter shifts emphasis toward rain and colder stops. This is a function-based starting point; no fixed temperature limit is inferred from a brand or fabric name. [DMC Mediterranean climate study, 2021](https://climatologia.meteochile.gob.cl/publicaciones/publicacionCambioClimatico/publicacionCambioClimatico202106001.pdf).

Vaude is a general trekking alternative; La Sportiva is a footwear shortlist. Cotton can be comfortable in hot dry lowlands but is a poor default for an exposed route with a cold or wet finish.

## Seasonal considerations

Summer means December–February here, not June–August. Winter frontal rain and snow at elevation require a different system.
