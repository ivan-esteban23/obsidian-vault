# Alpine

**Planning context:** Elevation, exposed terrain, wind and rapidly changing precipitation define the planning context. Conditions can change across a pass or between valley and summit. Climate labels here overlap: a maritime route can also be wet cold, and an alpine route can cross Mediterranean foothills.

## Weather characteristics

Elevation, exposed terrain, wind and rapidly changing precipitation define the planning context. Conditions can change across a pass or between valley and summit.

## Main risks

Exposure, precipitation changes, cold belays or rests, restricted movement in layered clothing, and mistaking equipment for technical competence.

## Recommended layering strategy

Choose a moisture-managed moving outfit, wind or storm protection that permits reach, and separate stop insulation. Check hood, gloves and layer compatibility before the route. This is a function-based starting point; no fixed temperature limit is inferred from a brand or fabric name. [Layering functions and exertion](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/layering-basics.html).

## Seasonal considerations

Summer does not remove snow or storm risk. Winter and shoulder-season travel can require skills and equipment beyond this trekking library.
