# High-Wind Trekking

For exposed walking where wind changes both heat loss and the ability to manage clothing and equipment. It applies to Patagonia and exposed Andean routes, with rain protection adjusted to the forecast.

## Functional kit

| Phase | Functions to cover |
| --- | --- |
| Moving | Light base, optional fleece or active insulation, and a wind or waterproof barrier appropriate to precipitation. |
| At a stop | Accessible static layer, protected head and hands, and a sheltered place when available. |
| Overnight | A suitable shelter fully pitched and supported according to its design, with independent dry storage. |

## Field sequence

1. Adjust the hood, cuffs and hem while still sheltered; ensure the hood does not block vision.
2. Secure loose straps and protect items that could blow away during a clothing change.
3. Use terrain and current guidance to decide whether to continue; there is no universal safe wind-speed limit for every person, surface or route.
4. At camp, choose a permitted site and use the shelter’s required guylines and anchors.

## Failure to avoid

A thick fleece beneath a poorly controlled shell can still lose heat through openings. A high-end jacket also cannot compensate for an unsuitable tent or unsafe footing.
