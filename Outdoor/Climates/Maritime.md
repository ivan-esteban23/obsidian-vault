# Maritime

**Planning context:** Ocean influence brings humid air, wind and changing rain, often with smaller temperature swings than a continental interior. This does not mean the weather is mild on an exposed coast. Climate labels here overlap: a maritime route can also be wet cold, and an alpine route can cross Mediterranean foothills.

## Weather characteristics

Ocean influence brings humid air, wind and changing rain, often with smaller temperature swings than a continental interior. This does not mean the weather is mild on an exposed coast.

## Main risks

Wind-driven rain through poorly adjusted openings, salt and moisture affecting maintenance, and gear that never dries fully.

## Recommended layering strategy

Prioritize reliable closures and rain coverage, then regulate heat with ventilation. A sea-level itinerary may need less moving insulation than a mountain route but still requires a protected stop layer. This is a function-based starting point; no fixed temperature limit is inferred from a brand or fabric name. ## Seasonal considerations

Summer can remain windy and wet. Winter adds cold and shorter daylight; latitude and elevation are more useful than the season label alone.
