# Dry Cold

**Planning context:** Cold air and relatively limited liquid precipitation favor lofted insulation, but wind, snow and moisture generated by the wearer still matter. Climate labels here overlap: a maritime route can also be wet cold, and an alpine route can cross Mediterranean foothills.

## Weather characteristics

Cold air and relatively limited liquid precipitation favor lofted insulation, but wind, snow and moisture generated by the wearer still matter.

## Main risks

Excess sweat freezing or chilling at stops, compressed insulation, cold ground, and treating dry weather as permission to omit weather protection.

## Recommended layering strategy

Keep the moving outfit lean enough to avoid sweating heavily. Add a much warmer static layer before resting. Protect down and sleep equipment from snow entry and internally generated moisture. This is a function-based starting point; no fixed temperature limit is inferred from a brand or fabric name. [Layering functions and exertion](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/layering-basics.html).

## Seasonal considerations

Winter and high-elevation nights increase reserve requirements. Dry regional averages do not exclude individual wet storms.
