# Spring

**Southern Hemisphere default:** September–November. These are meteorological planning months, not guaranteed snow, rain or park-opening dates. Northern Hemisphere use requires shifting the calendar and checking the local climate.

## Environmental characteristics

Longer days coexist with retreating snow, variable temperatures and wet transitions. In mountain terrain, lingering snow and saturated ground can matter more than the warmth in town. For Chile, distinguish [[Outdoor/Regional Guides/Central Chile Mediterranean Climate|Central Chile Mediterranean Climate]] from [[Outdoor/Regional Guides/Patagonia Region|Patagonia Region]] and the [[Outdoor/Regional Guides/Chilean Andes|Chilean Andes]]. [Chile national climate communication, 2016](https://mma.gob.cl/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/TCN-2016b1.pdf).

## Layering recommendations

Carry a flexible base/fleece system, reliable rain protection and a stop layer. Begin an ascent with fewer worn layers and adjust before sweating heavily. See [[Outdoor/Layering Systems/Moving vs Static Insulation|Moving vs Static Insulation]] for the transition between effort and rest.

## Common mistakes

Copying a dry summer packing list; overlooking snow on shaded routes; assuming a warm valley forecast describes a pass.
