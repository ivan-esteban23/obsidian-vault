# Wind and Rain Management

Wind protection, water resistance and waterproofness are different properties. A comfortable dry-wind layer can become inadequate when the same route receives sustained rain.

## How to decide

Use a wind layer for dry exposure when its ventilation suits the effort. Use an appropriately constructed waterproof shell for prolonged precipitation. Adjust hood, cuffs, hem and front opening before leaving a sheltered spot; the best fabric cannot compensate for an open collar facing wind-driven rain. [Rainwear selection](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/rainwear.html).

## Put it into practice

For camp, select a permitted sheltered site, secure the shelter as designed and keep critical insulation protected. A coat is not a reason to continue when wind threatens balance or the route becomes unsafe.

## Failure to avoid

Common mistake: treating a DWR finish or wax as equivalent to waterproof construction. G-1000 wax improves resistance; H₂No describes a performance standard for qualifying waterproof products.
