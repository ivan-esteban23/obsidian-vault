# Moving vs Static Insulation

Movement and rest create different insulation needs. A garment that is ideal during a windy lunch may cause excessive sweat during a sustained uphill approach.

## How to decide

Use the lightest practical moving combination for the current effort and exposure. Vent, remove insulation or reduce pace before the base becomes saturated. Add a protected static layer promptly at a stop, especially when wind and dampness combine. [Layering functions and exertion](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/layering-basics.html).

## Put it into practice

Keep the stop jacket and dry hand protection accessible. Decide whether the stop layer must fit over the shell or beneath it, then test that arrangement. In persistent rain, protect insulation from precipitation rather than assuming it will remain dry on the outside.

## Failure to avoid

Common mistake: judging warmth only at the trailhead. Test during a short loaded ascent and an exposed rest before committing to a multi-day system.
