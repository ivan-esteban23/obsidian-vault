# Rating Methodology

The three 1–10 scores are **editorial assessments**, not laboratory measurements, safety certifications, defect statistics or a universally comparable ranking. They are included to support browsing, with the limitations kept beside the numbers.

## Scope

Durability and technical performance apply to the profile’s linked specialties. Devold’s base-layer score is not a measure of shell performance; Scarpa’s footwear score does not rate a clothing system. Sustainability concerns documented practices and disclosure at the brand or explicitly identified product level.

## Anchored rubric

| Band | Durability within specialty | Technical performance within specialty | Sustainability practices and disclosure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Substantial evidenced limitations for intended use | Major evidenced mismatch to the stated role | Substantial evidenced shortcomings and little useful action |
| 3–4 | Limited construction or product-life support | Limited documented suitability | Narrow practices with major unresolved gaps |
| 5–6 | Adequate capability with important tradeoffs or limited support evidence | General capability or narrow evidence | Some specific practices; limited scope or outcome evidence |
| 7–8 | Strong intended-use construction and/or maintainability | Strong documented suitability in the stated specialty | Multiple specific practices or useful traceability/repair reporting |
| 9–10 | Exceptional maintainability or intended-use design supported by the checked evidence | Especially strong specialist fit to demanding tasks | Especially detailed, credible practices and transparency; not zero impact |

## How scores were assigned

Scores were assigned by reading the profile’s design/technical and responsibility sources, identifying its actual specialty, assessing it against these bands, and recording a rationale for each dimension. The source does not supply the editorial number. No weighted universal composite or overall winner is calculated. The profiles explain significant compromises such as light-fabric abrasion, wool care, service access and catalogue variation.

A lower score is not proof of a worse product or weaker ethics. Limited information increases uncertainty; absence of evidence is not treated as proof of poor real-world performance. Outdoor Research’s responsibility assessment, for example, has a narrower checked evidence base than a full corporate audit.

## Confidence and appropriate comparisons

Confidence in cited product functions is generally moderate; confidence in exact numerical differences is low. A one-point difference should not decide a purchase. Manufacturer testing, warranties and material claims are identified as such, not presented as independent comparative trials. No hands-on product tests were performed for this library.

The climate shortlists rank candidates for an explicitly stated clothing role. Specialist add-ons are discussed separately. Dataview matches are alphabetical unless a query explicitly sorts a named rating.

## Sustainability interpretation

Assess repair/longevity, material and chemical information, sourcing/animal-welfare practices, labor due diligence and reporting specificity. Certifications have limited scopes: animal-welfare certification does not measure total carbon impact, and membership in an initiative is not automatically product certification. Goals are distinguished from completed outcomes. Older reports retain their year and are not represented as current audits.

## Cost and fit

Budget, Midrange, Premium and Very Premium are qualitative positions within relevant categories, not Chilean prices. Cost and service vary by market. Fit is model-specific: no regional body-shape stereotype or remote size guarantee is used.

## Review triggers

Revisit a profile when a construction, fit, repair service, sourcing scheme or disclosure changes. Keep the previous evidence year visible when using an older report; update the static matrices if metadata or recommendations change.
