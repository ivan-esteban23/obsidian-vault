# Wet Cold Trekking

For exposed walking in persistent damp conditions where temperatures and drying opportunities make accumulated moisture consequential. Plan for the stop as well as the moving phase.

## Functional kit

| Phase | Functions to cover |
| --- | --- |
| While walking | Light moisture-managed base, adjustable insulation and a vented waterproof barrier. |
| At stops | Protected static layer and dry hand protection, added before the person becomes deeply chilled. |
| At camp | Separate wet and dry zones; keep sleep insulation and reserve clothing behind an independent waterproof barrier. |

## Field sequence

1. Inspect shell openings, seam condition and maintained water repellency before the trip.
2. Reduce sweat production through venting, pacing and removing worn insulation early.
3. Rotate or dry damp layers when realistic, without exposing all reserves to the same weather.
4. Account for cold ground with the mat as well as the bag, and reconsider the itinerary when the planned dry reserve is no longer reliable.

## Failure to avoid

No membrane guarantees that all sweat escapes. Synthetic insulation can be a useful wet-weather choice, but it still needs protection and does not justify deliberately soaking the whole system.
