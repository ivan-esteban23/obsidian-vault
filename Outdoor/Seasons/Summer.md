# Summer

**Southern Hemisphere default:** December–February. These are meteorological planning months, not guaranteed snow, rain or park-opening dates. Northern Hemisphere use requires shifting the calendar and checking the local climate.

## Environmental characteristics

Central Chile is commonly hot and dry, while Patagonian wind and mountain storms remain important. Long daylight is useful but does not make night temperatures or exposed passes benign. For Chile, distinguish [[Outdoor/Regional Guides/Central Chile Mediterranean Climate|Central Chile Mediterranean Climate]] from [[Outdoor/Regional Guides/Patagonia Region|Patagonia Region]] and the [[Outdoor/Regional Guides/Chilean Andes|Chilean Andes]]. [Chile national climate communication, 2016](https://mma.gob.cl/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/TCN-2016b1.pdf).

## Layering recommendations

Use light sun coverage and ventilation. Keep a separate wind layer and forecast-appropriate storm and stop reserves rather than wearing a thick waterproof layer all day. See [[Outdoor/Layering Systems/Moving vs Static Insulation|Moving vs Static Insulation]] for the transition between effort and rest.

## Common mistakes

Taking no gloves or insulation to a high pass; using a windshirt as rain protection; assuming northern Altiplano summer is always rain-free.
