# Three-Layer System

Think in functions: a base manages next-to-skin moisture, an insulating layer slows heat loss, and an outer layer manages wind or precipitation. Three functions do not require exactly three garments.

## How to decide

On a warm climb, a base layer may be the only worn garment. Wind exposure can justify adding a wind layer without insulation; a cold stop can require a static jacket over the moving outfit. A storm shell is added for weather protection rather than worn to satisfy a fixed formula. [Layering functions and exertion](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/layering-basics.html).

## Put it into practice

Build the kit around the coldest credible stop as well as the expected walking temperature. Place the layer likely to be needed next near the top of the pack. Test all closures and overlapping cuffs before departure.

## Failure to avoid

Common mistake: buying three expensive items that do not work together. A tight shell can compress insulation, while too much worn warmth can wet every layer with sweat.
